The strategy in 60 seconds

Five things to know

01

A joint DoH–HSE commitment

Commissioned together, and paired with a separate HSE AI Implementation Framework that handles the "how": governance, EU AI Act compliance and a project toolkit.

02

Four places AI will work

Clinical Care, Operations, Research & Innovation, and Public Health. Each has an objective, focus areas and named opportunities.

03

Humans stay in charge

Six principles anchor everything. The headline one: AI supports clinicians and never replaces clinical judgement.

04

A three-horizon roadmap

9 opportunities start in Year 1 ("Now"), 16 in Years 2–3 ("Next"), with scaling and newer ideas in Years 4–5. It is a living, directional guide.

05

Built on data and digital

Success depends on the EHR, National Shared Care Record, HSE Health App and the HSE's forthcoming Data Strategy, plus AI literacy for staff and public.

Why now

The pressure on Irish healthcare

Demand is rising faster than staff can be hired. The strategy frames AI as one way to deliver more care within current funding, not as a replacement for people.

STEP 4

Mapped to a horizon

Placed in Now, Next or Future. Detailed prioritisation happens later, under the Implementation Framework.

Value

  1. Patient: better outcomes and experience
  2. Staff: less admin burden, more productivity
  3. Organisation: system efficiency and lower risk

Feasibility

  1. Adoptability: clinical and operational uptake, leadership buy-in
  2. Capability: skills and capacity
  3. Availability: infrastructure, vendors and data

Reading between the lines

An honest read: what the strategy doesn't commit to

This is my assessment of the document, not part of it. It is a strong statement of direction; it is light on hard commitments.

No money attached

The implementation plan will need to be approved along with the necessary funding. There is no budget figure anywhere in the 45 pages.

Outcomes without targets

Five outcomes and example metrics (shorter waits, fewer admin hours), but no baselines and no numbers to hit by 2030. Success will be hard to judge.

The roadmap is a start list

Horizons say when exploration begins, and the roadmap is "subject to implementation planning and approval". Nothing is promised to be live by a date.

Built on foundations still being laid

It depends on the EHR, Shared Care Record, HSE Health App, the HSE Data Strategy and HIQA's AI guidance. The last two are still described as forthcoming.

Evidence is mostly borrowed

The strongest numbers come from a Swedish trial, one Irish hospital and an "up to 30%" supply-chain figure. Irish results at national scale don't exist yet.

A tension to watch

"Autonomous diagnostics" appears as a Year 4–5 idea, which sits uneasily with the human-in-the-loop principle. How that is resolved will matter for public trust.
